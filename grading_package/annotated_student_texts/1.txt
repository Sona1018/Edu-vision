Q1. Define Marketing. Explain nature of marketing.

Answer Marketing is the activity, set of institutions and processes for creating, communicating, delivering, and exchanging offerings that have value for customers, clients, partners, and society at large.

Nature of Marketing
• Customer-Centric Orientation: Modern marketing begins with understanding consumer behaivour, demand patterns and expectations. Organizations design products based on customer insights rather than merely focusing on production capabilities.

• Value Creation and Delivery:
-> Creating superior value through the marketing mix.
-> Focus on delivering benefits that exceed customer expectations and perceived cost.

• Exchange-Based
-> Marketing operates on the principle of voluntary 
-> exchange where both buyer and seller gain value.
-> It ensures mutual benefit and relationship development
• Continuous and Dynamic Process
-> It includes market research, product development, promotion, distribution and post-sale service.
-> It evolves with changes in technology and consumer preferences.


• Integrated activities

-> Marketing coordinates with production, finance, R&D and HR to achieve organizational objectives. It is a cross-functional activity.

Q4. Explain Marketing Environment.
Answer

Marketing environment refers to all internal and external factors that influence a company's marketing decisions and performance.

It affects pricing, product design, promotional strategy and distribution channels.



characteristics:
1. Dynamic in Nature
• Constantly changing due to technological, social and economic shifts.

2. Complex and Interrelated
• Different environmental factors influence each other
  Example: Technological change may affect social behaivour and economic growth.

3. Uncontrollable (External Factors)
• organizations cannot control political or economic conditions.
• They can only adapt strategies accordingly.

4. Requires Continuous Monitoring
• Environmental scanning helps firms identify opportunities and threats.
Types of Marketing Environment
• Internal Environment:

It refers to factors within the organization that influence marketing decisions and performance. These factors are largely controllable by management.

components:

-> Organizational Structure

• defines hierarchy, authority and responsibility.

->Company Objectives

• Marketing strategies must align with overall business goals.



-> Company Culture and Policies
  Organizational values influence brand image and customer relationships.
External Environment:
It consists of forces outside the organization that affect its marketing activities.
Types of external Environment
• Micro Environment

Forces close to the company that directly affect its operations.

• Macro Environment

Broader societal forces affecting all organizations.

Q5. Explain Micro and Macro External Environmental Factors.
Answer:
Micro Environment:
• Refer to forces close to the company that directly affect its operations.
• These factors influence day-to-day business decisions.
Components:

1. customers
-> Target market segments who purchase products.
-> Their preferences directly affect demand.
2. Competitors
-> Rival firms offering similar products.
-> Influence pricing, quality standards and innovation.



3. suppliers
-> Provide raw materials, components or services.
-> Their reliability affects production and cost.
4. Marketing Intermediaries
-> Distributors, wholesalers, retailers.
-> Help in product distribution and availability.
5. Publics

-> Media, financial institutions, government agencies and local communities.
Macro Environment:
• Refers to broader societal forces affecting all industries.
• Commonly analyzed using the PESTLE framework.
1. Political Factors

- Government policies, taxation, trade regulation, political stability.

2. Economic Factors

• Inflation rate, GDP growth, interest rates, unemployment levels.

3. Social Factors

• Demographics, cultural values, lifestyle changes, education level.

4. Technological Factors

• Innovation, automation, artificial intelligence, digital transformation.



5. Legal Factors

• Consumer protection laws, labor laws, environmental regulation.

6. Environmental Factors

• Climate change, sustainability concerns, environmental policies
