Q1 Define marketing. Explain nature of marketing.

Definition of Marketing

Marketing is the process of identifying 
customer needs and wants, creating value
and delivering the value in exchange of 
something beneficial to the organization.

According to Philip Kotler :
"Marketing is a social and managerial
 process by which individuals and groups
 obtain what they need and want through 
 creating and exchanging value with others"

In simple words:
Marketing is not just selling. It is about
understanding customer needs, wants, creating
product/service accordingly and building
long-term relationships.

Nature of Marketing

1. Marketing is Customer-Oriented

Modern marketing starts with customer needs 
and ends with customer satisfaction.
Businesses conduct:
• Market research0
• Consumer behaviour analysis
• Demand forecasting

Eg Zomato studies customer preferences (faster delivery 
discounts, cuisines) and designs its app accordingly

2. Marketing is a Value-Creation Process
It creates value by:
• Designing suitable products
• Setting appropriate prices
• Ensuring availability
• Communicating benefits

Value = Benefit received - Cost paid

Eg Apple provides superior design, ecosystem integration 
and brand prestige, creating high perceived value.

3. Marketing is a Continuous Process

It does not end with a sale. It includes:
• After-sales service
• Customer support
• Relationship building
• Feedback Management

Eg Amazon follows up with customers, allows easy
returns and collect ratings to improve service
This reflects the concept of Relationship
Marketing

4. Marketing is an exchange process

At the heart of marketing lies exchange.
It involves at least two parties, something
of value, willingness to exchange and
ability to communicate.

customer pays money → company gives product
exchange can also be of ideas, services,
learning or experiences.

Eg NGO market social cause, where people exchange
money for social satisfaction.

5. Marketing is both an art and science

Science: market research, consumer analytics,
pricing strategies, demand estimation

Art: Branding, advertising creativity, emotional
positioning.

Eg Apple uses creative, impactful advertisements (art)
along with strong distribution network (science).

6. Marketing is goal-oriented

The objectives of marketing include:
• Profit maximization
• Market share growth
• Customer satisfaction
• Brand loyalty

Acc to HKTG: A South Asian perspective with
consumer, marketing aligns customer satisfaction
with organizational objectives.

7 Marketing operates in Dynamic environment

Marketing decisions are influenced by
• economic environment
• political environment
• social and cultural changes
• Technological advancements

Eg During COVID-19, companies shifted to online
marketing and e-commerce.

8 Marketing is Integrated and Interdisciplinary

Marketing works with
• Production
• Finance
• Human resources
• supply chain

It influences and is influenced by other departments

→ Marketing is more than selling. It is a customer
oriented, value-driven, continuous exchange process
that aims to satisfy needs while achieving
organizational goals. Modern marketing focuses on
building long-term relationships rather than short
term profits.

Q3 Differentiate between needs and wants.
Explain how marketing influences both.

According to Philip Kotler, human needs are
the most basic concept underlying marketing.
It begins with identifying needs and then
shaping wants into demand.

1. Needs

Needs are basic human requirements necessary
for survival and well-being. They are not
created by marketers, they are inherent in
human beings.

Eg: Food, clothing, shelter, safety, social
belonging, esteem

• Hunger is a need
• Protection from weather is a need
• Communication is a need.
Need are universal and limited in number

2. Wants

Wants are the form that needs take as
shaped by culture, personality and individual
preferences.

They are influenced by social environment,
income level, culture, lifestyle, advertising

Eg:
• Hunger (need) → Pizza / Burger (want)
• Communication (need) → iPhone (want)
• Transportation (need) → SUV car (want)
Wants are unlimited and vary from person to person

Difference between Need and Want

| Basis        | Need                      | Want                                |
| ------------ | ------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| 1. Meaning   | Basic human requirements. | Specific form of satisfying a need. |
| 2. Nature    | Limited & Universal       | Unlimited and Personal              |
| 3. Origin    | Arise naturally           | Influenced by society and marketing |
| 4. Necessity | Essential for survival    | Not essential for survival          |
| 5. Example   | Clothing                  | Branded clothes - Nike              |

How marketing influence needs and wants

1. Marketing Doesn't create Needs
It cannot create basic needs like hunger or
safety. They already exist.
Eg: People need food regardless of marketing activities.

2. Marketing influences wants
It shapes how consumers choose to satisfy
their needs.

Eg A person needs refreshment. Through advertising
Coca-Cola positions its drink as happiness
enjoyment. Thus consumers starts wanting Coca-Cola.
instead of plain water.
here, marketing influenced the want, not the need.

3. Marketing converts Want into Demands
When wants are backed by purchasing power,
they become demand.

Eg A student wants smartphone. Through promotional
campaigns, Apple Inc creates desire for iPhones.
If student has money, want becomes demand.

4. Marketing creates Perceived Value
It highlights:
• Quality
• Brand image
• Emotional appeal
• Status symbols

Eg Luxury brand markets products as symbols of
status and success. This influences customer
preferences through towards premium choices.

5. It shapes Lifestyle and Aspirations
Advertising often connects products with:
• Success
• Beauty
• Confidence
• Happiness

Eg Fitness campaigns by Nike encourage active
lifestyle aspirations. Thus it plays a
psychological role in shaping wants.

6. Role of culture and society

Wants are often deeply influenced by cultural
and social environments.

Eg: In India, festive marketing campaigns increase
desire for good jewellery and new clothes during
Diwali. Thus, marketing aligns wants with cultural values.

Q4 Explain marketing environment.

Marketing does not operate in isolation. Every
business functions within a set of forces and
conditions that influence its environment
performance. This force collectively form the
marketing environment.

According to Philip Kotler, the marketing env.
consists of the actors and forces that affect
a company's ability to develop and maintain
successful relationships with customers.

Thus it refers to all internal and external
factors that influences marketing decisions and activities.
It includes:
• Customers
• Competitors
• Suppliers
• Government
• Economic conditions
• Social and cultural forces
• Technological developments
These forces creates both opportunities and
threats for businesses. Marketing managers
must continuously monitor and adapt to
these environmental changes.

Characteristics of Marketing Environment

1. Dynamic in nature

It is continuously changing changes in technology,
customer preferences, and economic conditions
requires business to adapt continuously.

Eg: The rise of digital platforms transformed
traditional marketing into social media advertising.

2. Complex

It consists of many interrelated factors. A
change in one factor affects others.

Eg: Economic slowdown affects purchasing power,
which impacts sales and production.

3. Uncontrollable forces

Most environmental forces are beyond the control
of the company.

Eg:
• Govt policies
• Regulations
• Natural disasters

Companies can only adjust their strategies according.

4. Relative and Context Specific

Marketing env. differs across countries and industries
Eg Marketing env. in India differ from those in
developed countries due to cultural and income
differences.

5 Create Opportunities and Threats

Environmental changes may benefit some companies
while harming others.

Eg Growth of e-commerce created opportunities for
Amazon but challenged traditional retailers

Factors Affecting Marketing Environment

1. Economic Factors
Includes income levels, inflation, interest rates and
economic growth. They influence consumer purchasing
power.

2. Technological Factors
New technologies change production, distribution and
communication methods

Eg Online payment systems transformed retail business.

3. Social and Cultural Factors
Includes values, traditions, lifestyle and beliefs.
They influence buying behaviour.

Eg Growing health awareness increases demand for
organic products.

4. Political and Legal Factors
Govt regulations, tax policies and consumer protection
laws affects marketing decisions
5. Competitive factors
Presence of competitors influences pricing,
promotion and marketing/product strategy.

Eg: Competition between Coca-cola and Pepsi leads
to aggressive marketing campaigns.

6. Natural factors
Availability of natural resources and environmental
issues affect marketing and production.

Eg Climate change has increased focus on
sustainable marketing.

Importance of Marketing environment

1. Helps in Identifying opportunities
Companies can identify new markets and
emerging trends.

2. Reduces Risks
By analysing env. changes, businesses can avoid
unexpected losses.

3. Aids in Strategic Planning
Marketing strategies are designed based on
environmental analysis.

4. Ensures Business Survival
Companies that fail to adapt to env. changes
may lose competitiveness.

5. Improves Customer Satisfaction
Understanding trends and economic conditions helps
in better product design.

Q5 Explain micro and macro external environmental
factors

Micro External Environmental Factors

These include forces close to the company that
directly affect its marketing operations and
customer relationships.
These factors are external but closely linked
to the company and are partially influenceable

1. Suppliers
They provide raw materials, components and
services required for the production.
• Any delay or price affects cost and profitability.
• Dependence on limited suppliers increases risk

Eg Shortage of semi-conductor chips affected production
of automobile and smartphone companies worldwide.

2. Marketing Intermediaries
These are firms that help in promoting, selling
and distributing products. These include:
• Wholesalers
• Retailers
• Logistic providers
• Advertising agencies

Eg Amazon depends heavily on delivery partners and
warehouse networks to ensure timely service.

3. Customers
It is the most important factor in micro-environment
offered types of market:
• Consumer market
• Business market
• Govt market
• International market
Companies must study customer preferences
and purchasing behaviour carefully.

Eg: Hindustan Unilever Limited offers small sachet
packaging for rural customers to match their
purchasing power.

4. Competitors

They influence pricing, product quality and
promotional strategies. Businesses must continue-
ly monitor competitors actions.

Eg: Coca-Cola and PepsiCo compete through aggressive
advertising and pricing strategies.

5. Publics

They are groups that have an interest or
impact on the organisation. They include:
• Media
• Financial institutions
• Govt agencies
• Local communities
• General public

Negative media coverage can damage brand
reputation.
Macro External Environmental Factors

It consists of large societal forces that
affect all organisation in an industry. These
forces are generally uncontrollable and long-term
in nature.

1. Demographic factors
This include:
• Population Size
• Age structure
• Gender distribution
• Income levels
• education

Eg: India large young population increase demand
for digital services and smartphones.

2. Economic factors
• Inflation
• Interest rates
• Economic growth
• Purchasing power

During economic slowdown, consumers reduce spending
on luxury goods.

3. Natural factors
• Availability of natural resources
• Environmental pollution
• Climate change

Eg: Tata Motors promotes electric vehicles due to
increasing environmental concerns.
4. Technological factors

It affects distribution, production and comm.
Technological advancement creates both threat
and opportunities

Eg: Digital transformation enabled companies to use
social media and online sales platforms.

5 Political and legal factors

This include:
• Govt regulations
• Tax policies
• Trade policies
• Consumer protection laws

Eg: Changes in GST rates directly impact
pricing strategies.

6 Socio-cultural factors

This include:
• Values
• Beliefs
• Customs
• Lifestyle Trends

Eg Growing health consciousness → low sugar
products
