3. Differentiate between "needs" and "wants". Explain how
marketing influences both of them.

Sol Human beings have basic requirements essential for survival,
while their desires for particular products or services are shaped
by culture, lifestyle and personality.
Marketing helps in efficiently fulfilling needs and in creating or
shaping wants among consumers.

 BASIS                        NEEDS                    WANTS 
 
 Meaning          Basic human requirements       Desires for specific products/
                  essential for survival.        services shaped by culture, 
                                                 lifestyle & personality.

# Marketing Influence on Needs.

Although needs are natural and cannot be created,
marketing significantly influences how they are satisfied,
making products more appealing, accessible and effective
for consumers.


1. Product Variety - Companies offer multiple options to meet the 
same need.
Eg: Hunger can satisfied with rice, bread or ready-to-eat meals.

2. Accessibility & Convenience - Ensuring essential products are 
easy to obtain.
Eg: Grocery delivery apps like Big Basket make food readily available.

3. Quality & Efficiency - Highlighting improved features, safety & reliability.
Eg: Water purifier ads emphasize safe drinking water & hygiene.

4. Awareness & Education - Informing consumers about healthier or
 better ways to fulfill needs.
Eg: Marketing nutritious cereals encourages people to meet food 
requirements in a healthier way.

5. Pricing & Affordability - Ensuring essential products are within
 reach of different income groups
Eg: Tata Nano provides affordable transportation for middle-
class families.

# Marketing Influence on Wants.

Wants are shaped and often created by marketing. Marketers 
use various techniques to convert needs into specific desires,
 influencing consumer preferences and choices.

1. Advertising & Branding (Promotion + Positioning)

* Persuades consumers to desire specific brands while 
associating them with prestige, lifestyle or emotions.
Eg: Rolex watches advertised as symbols of success, Nike 
sportswear promoted to youth for fitness and style.

2. Packaging & Design - Attractive visuals and appealing 
design increase desire.
Eg: Colourful snack packs appeal to children's tastes.

3. Lifestyle & Experience Association - Marketing links products
 to aspirational living or emotional benefits.
Eg: BMW or Mercedes ads associate luxury cars with status & success

4. Celebrity Endorsements & Influencers - Influence social trends
& create aspirational
appeal.
Eg: iPhones promoted by celebrities encourage desire beyond
basic communication needs.

5. Psychological Triggers - Appeals to emotions such as pride,
enjoyment, security or social recognition
Eg: Ads showing family happiness with a particular appliance
create emotional desire.

[Q_START: Q4 | Type: Analytical_Explanation | Weight: 20.0%]
4. Explain Marketing Environment.

Soln # Meaning
-> Marketing Environment refers to all internal and external
forces that influence a firm's ability to develop and
maintain successful relationships with customers.
-> Marketing Environment = All factors inside and outside
a business that affect marketing decisions.
-> Most factors are uncontrollable, so marketers must adapt
rather than control them.
Eg: A rise in fuel prices increase transportation costs,
affect product pricing, influence customer demand.

# Characteristics.

1. Dynamic
-> Environment is continuously changing and changes can
be slow or sudden.
Eg: Rapid increase in digital payments after UPI
adoption.

2. Uncertain
-> Future changes are difficult to predict accurately.
Eg: COVID-19 drastically consumer buying behaviour.

3. Uncontrollable
-> External forces cannot be controlled.
-> Firms can only respond and adapt.
4. Complex
-> Multiple inter-related factors influence business 
simultaneously.
Eg: A new government policy can affect prices, demand 
and competition together.

# Types of Marketing Environment.

1. Internal Environment
-> Consists of factors within the organization that influence
 marketing decisions.
-> These factors are partially controllable.
-> Key Components

2. Top Management - determines company vision, mission
 and objectives.

3. Organizational Structure - defines authority, responsibility 
and coordination within firm.

4. Company Culture - shared values and beliefs that 
guide employee behaviour.

5. Resources - financial, human, technological and physical 
resources available to the company.

6. Internal Policies - includes pricing, HR, marketing policies
-> Eg: Tata Motors uses strong management, flexible structure, 
customer-focused culture and R&D to launch
 products like the TATA Nano, meeting customer needs 
effectively.

2. External Environment.
-> Includes all factors outside the organization that 
influence marketing decisions.
-> These factors are uncontrollable, so companies 
must adapt to them to remain competitive.
1. Micro Environment
-> Comprises forces close to the company that directly 
affect its ability to serve customers.
-> Influence day-to-day operations, product availability, 
pricing & customer satisfaction.

2. Macro Environment
-> Includes larger societal forces that affect the entire 
industry.
-> Shape overall market trends, demand patterns 
and long-term strategies.

-> Eg: Amazon India adapts to changing customer preferences (micro),
 supplier availability, competition from Flipkart and
 government regulations like GST (macro) to make strategic 
marketing decisions.

# Micro Environment.

-> Consists of external forces close to the company that directly 
affect its ability to serve customers.
-> These factors impact day-to-day operations, pricing,
 product availability, promotion and overall customer satisfaction.

1. Customers
-> People or organizations who buy the company's products.
-> Shifts in preferences, needs, income or buying habits
 impact decisions.
Eg: Byju's targets students and parents based on demand for
 online education.

2. Suppliers
-> Firms or individuals providing raw materials, components 
or services.
-> Price hikes, delays, quality issues or shortages affect
production and costs.
Eg: Global chip shortage disrupted automobile manufacturing.

3. Competitors
-> Companies offering similar products or services.
-> New product launches, pricing strategies, promotions or
innovations affect decisions.
Eg: Coca-Cola vs Pepsi competing on price, branding & taste.

4. Marketing Intermediaries
-> Organizations helping in promotion, distribution and sales.
-> Changes in channel efficiency, delivery speed or promotional
support impact decisions.
Eg: Amazon ensures smooth connection between sellers
and buyers.

5. Publics
-> Groups with interest or impact on the organization.
-> Includes financial publics(investors), media, government,
local communities and general public.
-> Investor expectations, media coverage, government
regulations or community reactions.
Eg: Investors demanding better returns influence corporate
strategy; negative media coverage can harm
brand image.

# Macro Environment

-> Consists of larger societal forces that affect the entire
industry.
-> These are uncontrollable and influence long-term
strategies, demand patterns and market trends.

1. Demographic Environment
-> Population characteristics such as age, gender, income,
education, occupation, family size impact marketing
decisions.
Eg: India's young population increases demand for smartphones
and online education.

2. Economic Environment
-> Economic conditions affecting consumer's purchasing 
power and spending habits.
-> Include inflation, recession, interest rates, repo rates
 or economic growth.
Eg: Inflation reduces spending on luxury goods.

3. Political & Legal Environment
-> Government policies, laws, and regulations affecting 
business operations.
-> New tax laws, trade regulations, consumer protection 
rules or labour laws affect decisions.
Eg: GST implementation affected pricing & supply chains.

4. Technological Environment
-> Innovations in products, processes & marketing 
methods.
-> Include automation, digitalization or new product 
development.
Eg: AI & data analytics allow personalized campaigns.

5. Social & Cultural Environment
-> Society's values, beliefs, customs & lifestyles.
-> Shifts in social trends, health consciousness or consumer 
ethics affect decisions.
Eg: Growing health awareness increases demand for 
organic foods.

6. Natural Environment
-> Ecological factors, climate & natural resources.
-> Resource scarcity, climate change & environmental 
laws impact decisions greatly.
Eg: Plastic bans encourage companies to adopt 
eco-friendly packaging.
