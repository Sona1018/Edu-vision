Q Define Marketing. Explain nature of marketing.

Ans:- Marketing is the process of identifying customer needs and
wants, developing products or services to satisfy those needs, pricing
them appropriately, promoting them effectively, and making
them available at the right place and time.
In simple words,
Marketing = Understanding customers + Creating value +
Delivering satisfaction.
Marketing is not just selling. It begins before production and
continues even after sale through customer service and
feedback.
Philip Kotler defines: "Marketing is a social and managerial
process by which individuals and groups
obtain what they need and want through creating and
exchanging products and value with others."
American Marketing Association (AMA) defines: "Marketing is the
activity, set of
institutions, and processes for creating, communicating, delivering,
and exchanging offerings that have value for customers,
clients, partners, and society at large".
eg: Zomato does not just deliver food.
It studies eating habits, identifies convenience as a major
need, provides fast delivery, discounts, ratings, and personalized
recommendations.
This entire process is marketing - not just selling food.

Nature of Marketing:
The nature of marketing explains its basic characteristics
and features.
① Customer-Centric
Marketing starts and ends with the customer.




• Focus is on customer satisfaction.
• Profit is the result of satisfying customers.
eg: Amazon focuses on fast delivery, easy returns, and customer
reviews to ensure satisfaction.

② Continuous Process
Marketing is an ongoing activity.
• Customer preferences change.
• Competition changes.
• Technology evolves.
eg: Mobile phone brands launch new models every year to meet
changing customer demands.

③ Integrated Activity
Marketing involves coordination of the 4 P's of Marketing:
• Product
• Price
• Promotion
• Place
All these elements must work together.
eg: Apple Inc. maintains premium pricing that matches its
high-quality products and luxury brand image.

④ Value-Oriented
Marketing focuses on creating value, not just selling products.
eg: Netflix provides:
• Entertainment
• Convenience
• Personalized recommendations.
This combination creates customer value.

⑤ Exchange-Based
Marketing is based on exchange.
• Customer gives money
• Company provides product or service
• Both parties benefit
Without exchange, marketing cannot exist.



Q What all are scope of marketing and importance of marketing.

Ans:- Marketing today has evolved from simple advertising into a dynamic, data-driven discipline that covers everything from product creation to long-term customer care.

Scope of Marketing

* The scope refers to "what is marketed" and the broad range of activities involved in the process.
• Market Research: Gathering and analyzing data on customer needs, competitor moves, and market trends to make informed decisions.
• Product Planning & Development: Designing and improving products based on customer feedback to ensure they meet a real demand.
• Branding: Creating a unique identity—including logos, taglines, and values—that distinguishes a business from competitors.
• Pricing Strategies: Determining a product's price by balancing production costs, competitor rates, and what customers are willing to pay.
• Promotion & Communication: Using digital (social media, SEO, email) and traditional (TV, print) channels to create awareness and drive action.
• Distribution (Place): Selecting the right channels—online stores, retail outlets, or third-party marketplaces—to ensure products are easily accessible.
• Customer Relationship Management (CRM): Managing post-sale interactions to build long-term loyalty and turn buyers into brand advocates.



• Digital Marketing: Leveraging modern platforms and technologies like AI to target niche audience with hyper-personalized content.
• Diverse Entities: Marketing is not limited to physical goods, it extends to services, events, experiences, persons (celebrities), places (tourism), properties, organizations, information, and ideas.

Importance of Marketing

- Marketing is the "heartbeat" of a business, essential for both immediate survival and long-term growth.
• Drives Sales and Revenue: By attracting potential customers and encouraging them to purchase, marketing directly impacts a company's financial performance.
• Builds Brand Awareness: It serves as a "megaphone" to the world, ensuring that a brand stays top-of-mind and recognizable in a crowded market.
• Fosters Customer Loyalty: Effective marketing builds trust and emotional connections, which are five times cheaper to maintain than acquiring new customers.
• Provides Market Insights: Constant research allows businesses to anticipate shifts in consumer behavior and adapt their strategies to remain relevant.
• Enhances Competitive Advantage: Targeted marketing highlights a brand's unique value proposition, helping it stand out against competitors.


• Supports Business Expansion: It identifies new growth opportunities, such as untapped customer segments or new geographical markets.
• Promotes Innovation: By identifying unmet needs, marketing acts as a "secret weapon" that guides the development of ground-breaking new products.
• Societal Impact: On a larger scale, marketing raises the standard of living by providing a wide variety of goods, creating jobs, and encouraging social responsibility.



Q Differentiate between needs and wants. Explain how marketing influences the above.

Ans:- In the world of marketing, the distinction between a need and a want is the foundation of how brands communicate with us. While they sound similar, they operate on very different psychological levels.

| Feature | Needs | Wants |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Definition | Basic requirements for human survival and functioning | Desires for specific things to satisfy a need. |
| Nature | Universal and physiological (Biological) | Culturally and personally shaped (Psychological). |
| Intensity | Essential, non-fulfillment can lead to illness or death. | Non-essential; life continues without them. |
| Examples | Food, shelter, water, clothing, security. | A steak dinner, Fiji water, a mansion, designer labels. |

How Marketing Influences Needs and Wants

- It is a common misconception that "marketing creates needs". In reality, marketing identifies needs and then shapes wants.

1. Transformation (The "Bridge" Creator)
Marketing takes a basic, generic need and bridges it to a specific brand.



• The Need: You are thirsty (physiological).
• The Influence: Marketing uses imagery of refreshment and taste to make you want a Coca-Cola specifically, rather than just a glass of tap water.

2. Social and Cultural Shaping
Marketers study the culture and social circles of their target audience. They position products not just as tools, but as symbols of status or belonging.
eg: You need a way to communicate with your family. Marketing influences you to want the latest iPhone to feel part of a specific social group or "lifestyle."

3. Artificial Scarcity and Urgency
- Through tactics like "Limited Editions" or "Flash Sales", marketing heightens the desire for a want, making it feel as urgent as a need. This triggers a psychological response (FOMO - Fear of Missing Out) that overrides logical decision-making.

4. Problem Awareness

* Sometimes we have a "latent need" (a problem we didn't know we had). Marketing brings this to our attention.
eg: You didn't know you needed an air purifier until marketing educated you on indoor air quality. Now you want a specific HEPA filter brand to solve that newly discovered need.



5. Emotional Association
Marketing shifts a product from a functional item to an emotional one.
• The Need: Transportation.
• The Marketing Influence: A Volvo marketing campaign focuses on "safety", turning a car into a way to "protect your family". You no longer just want a car, you want the feeling of security that brand promises.




Q. Explain marketing environment.

Ans:- The marketing environment consists of all internal and external factors that affect a company's ability to build and maintain successful relationships with its target customers. These factors can present either opportunities for growth or threats to a business's operations.

1. Internal Environment (Controllable)
These are factors within the organization that marketing managers can directly manage or influence.
• The 5Ms: Men (employees), Money (financial resources), Machinery (equipment), Materials (raw materials), and Minutes (time).
• Corporate Culture: The values and norms that guide team collaboration.
• Organizational Structure: How departments like Finance, R&D, and HR coordinate with the marketing team.
2. Micro Environment (Semi-controllable)
Also known as the Task Environment, these are external actors closely linked to the company that directly impact its daily operations.
• Customers: The most important factor; companies must monitor their changing needs and preferences.
• Competitors: Other firms offering similar products; businesses must track their strategies to maintain a competitive edge.
• Suppliers: Providers of raw materials and resources; disruptions here can halt production.
• Marketing Intermediaries: Wholesalers, retailers, and agencies that help promote and distribute goods.




• Publics: Any group with an interest in the company, such as the media, financial institutions, or local communities.

3. Macro Environment (Uncontrollable)
These are larger societal forces that affect the entire industry. They are often analyzed using the PESTLE framework.
• Political: Government policies, trade restrictions, and political stability.
• Economic: Factors like inflation, interest rates, and consumer purchasing power.
• Social & Cultural: Societal values, lifestyle trends, and cultural norms.
• Technological: Innovations like AI or e-commerce that can make products obsolete or create new markets.
• Legal: Laws regarding consumer protection, labor, and health and safety.
• Environmental (Natural): Natural resource availability, pollution, and climate change.

Why it Matters
• Identifying Opportunities: Spotting trends early (e.g., a shift toward eco-friendly products) allows for a "first-mover advantage".
• Mitigating Risks: Anticipating threats (eg : a new competitor or regulation) helps businesses prepare contingency plans.
• Strategic Planning: Understanding these factors is essential for making informed decisions on pricing, product development, and resource allocation.




Q. Explain Micro and Macro external environmental factors.

Ans:- The external marketing environment is divided into micro and macro factors. These represent the immediate and broad forces that a business cannot fully control but must navigate to succeed.

1. Micro Environment (Task Environment)
These are "close-to-home" actors that directly impact a company's ability to serve its customers and manage its daily operations.
• Customers: The most critical factor. Businesses must track their changing needs and purchasing behaviour, as their satisfaction drives long-term success.
• Competitors: Other firms offering similar products. Monitoring their pricing, product features, and marketing strategies is essential for maintaining a competitive edge.
• Suppliers: Provide the raw materials and resources, needed for production. Any delays or price hikes here can directly halt a business's supply chain.
• Marketing Intermediaries: Third-party partners like wholesalers, retailers, and logistics firms that help move products from the company to the final consumer.
• Publics: Groups that have an interest in or impact on the company, such as local communities, media outlets, and financial institutions (banks/investors).
2. Macro Environment (General Environment)
These are large-scale societal forces that affect not just one company, but the entire industry and


economy. They are typically analyzed using the PESTLE framework.
• Political: Government policies, trade tariffs, labor laws, and political stability in the regions where a business operates.
• Economic: Broad financial factors like inflation, interest rates, exchange rates, and the general purchasing power of consumers.
• Social & Cultural: Trends in lifestyle, demographics (age, education), and cultural values that influence what people buy (eg. a shift toward health-conscious foods).
• Technological: Innovations such as AI, automation, and digital infrastructure that can create new market opportunities or make current products obsolete.
• Legal: Specific laws a business must follow, including consumer protection, health and safety regulations, and intellectual property rights.
• Environment (Natural): Ecological factors like climate change, scarcity of raw materials, and government-mandated sustainability practices.
